### **think2earn: self funding verifiable open hardware**

#### **1. The Problem: Why Wellness Apps Fail & Hardware is Unfundable**

My point of building this is to solve two problems that kill innovation in personalized health:

*   **Behavioral Inertia:** Wellness apps are a revolving door. They fail to build lasting habits because their rewards are superficial and have no real value, leading to massive user churn. I want to reward users with discounts on open hardware think2earn manufactures. 
*   **Unfundable Hardware:** Good hardware, like our non-invasive glucose monitor, is nearly impossible to fund. It requires hundreds of thousands in R&D, manufacturing, and clinical trials before you can sell "medical" units. This execution risk is too high for most investors.

#### **2. Our Solution: Use Software to Pay for Hardware**

We're not trying to raise a massive hardware seed round. My strategy is to de-risk the entire project by launching a revenue-generating mobile app first.

*   **Phase 1 (Immediate Revenue): Launch the "Tamagotchi for Wellness" App.** We're shipping a gamified wellness app on NEAR. Users subscribe to engage in on-chain commitments like `Proof of Rest`. This creates an immediate SaaS revenue stream and builds the health-conscious community we need.
*   **Phase 2 (Self-Funded R&D): Build the Backend & Economic Engine.** We use the profits from the app to fund the hard tech: building the decentralized storage layer on Swarm and the AVS-like "Impact Evaluator" on NEAR for rewarding data contributions.
*   **Phase 3 (Captive Market Upsell): Launch the Hardware.** We'll offer our clinically validated fNIRS device directly to our most engaged app users—a pre-built, high-intent customer base that wants what we're building. This completely de-risks the hardware launch.
